# Hero notes for the coach: game knowledge confirmed by players, sent to Claude when the coached
# player played that hero. Format: hero|note. A hero can have several lines. Names that the
# scoreboard can show for the same hero are separated by "/" (for example Hulk/Bruce Banner).
# Keep notes about how a hero plays and how to read their stats. Avoid exact numbers and ability
# effects that change with balance patches.
# Drafted from community guides (Mobalytics, Aim Lab) and corrected by the user.
# Last checked: Season 10.5 (October 2026).

# ---- Vanguards ----
Magneto|A main shield tank, not a brawler. He's strongest at mid range; his slow projectiles miss fast or small targets, so low accuracy often means shooting at mobile targets or from the wrong range.
Magneto|His damage blocked comes from his self-bubble, the bubble he puts on an ally, and Metallic Curtain. Good players save the ally bubble for divers and key moments, and don't hold the curtain up when nothing is hitting it, because it drains anyway.
Magneto|A fully charged Mag-Cannon knocks enemies back, which is his answer to melee divers. Deaths to divers often mean the knockback or self-bubble was already spent.
Magneto|His ultimate, Meteor M, absorbs enemy projectiles, so it's best timed to eat an enemy ultimate or heavy projectile fire.

Doctor Strange|A main shield tank with brawling abilities; damage blocked is his key stat. When the shield breaks it has to recharge from empty, so good players drop it between hits and save it for stuns and ultimates.
Doctor Strange|He's weak against brawlers such as The Thing, Hulk and Wolverine, who bypass his shield, and dive heroes can simply go around him. High deaths often mean he was caught up close with a low shield or with levitation already used. Check the enemy team for these heroes.
Doctor Strange|Maelstrom of Madness charges from his hits, so low damage can mean he isn't attacking between shield uses.
Doctor Strange|His ultimate, Eye of Agamotto, stuns a group but has a slow startup, so it's best from a flank, from high ground or after levitating in.

Hulk/Bruce Banner|A brawl tank who can dive, not a pure dive tank like Venom. His forms (Bruce Banner, Hero Hulk, and Monster Hulk from the ultimate) are combined on the scoreboard.
Hulk/Bruce Banner|When Hulk dies he "de-mechs" into Bruce Banner, and transforms back into Hulk once his ultimate charges enough. As Bruce he has the Gamma Ray Gun and the Gamma Grenade; landing his shots and the grenade charges his ultimate, which speeds up getting back into Hulk, so accurate play as Bruce matters.
Hulk/Bruce Banner|Indestructible Guard (the bubble) shields him and nearby allies; good players save it for enemy ultimates and to save teammates, not for small damage. Gamma Burst (the clap) adds ranged damage and recharges the guard.
Hulk/Bruce Banner|Radioactive Lockdown is used to stun out-of-position targets and to cancel enemy ultimates. Incredible Leap also stuns flying enemies it hits. Neither shows on the scoreboard.
Hulk/Bruce Banner|His ultimate turns him into Monster Hulk: much more health, and the clap does more damage on a shorter cooldown. He keeps his stun but loses the bubble during the ultimate, and gains a grab that smashes an enemy and stuns them so his team can focus them down. His biggest weakness as Monster Hulk is his size: he's an easy target for the enemy to focus down.
Hulk/Bruce Banner|Common mistake: staying too long in fights he can't win, which soaks up healing and builds the enemy's ultimates. He's weak against chained crowd control. He can't be hurt while transforming (Bruce to Hulk, or Hulk to Monster Hulk), so good players use the transformation to dodge burst damage or an enemy ultimate. Good Hulks also play aggressively before using the ultimate, because transforming gives them extra health.
Thor|A brawler like Hulk, but more of a diver because of his high burst damage. He's also a strong protector of his backline, because he's consistently one of the best 1v1 heroes. KOs, final hits and damage matter more for him than for shield tanks; low damage blocked is expected.
Thor|Every ability spends Thorforce, which refills when his attacks land. Diving with no Thorforce leaves him no escape, so high deaths often come from diving on empty.
Thor|Awakening Rune is his burst for finishing low-health targets. It can be cancelled, refunding 1 Thorforce, which good players use to get out.
Thor|He's weak against heavy crowd control. He can't be stunned while casting his ultimate, so it can counter enemy stun ultimates.

The Thing|Purely a brawler: he can't be knocked back or launched, so he walks straight into the enemy, and his punches go through shields, which makes him strong against shield tanks such as Doctor Strange. His damage blocked can be about the same as other tanks'. His accuracy doesn't reflect his play: it isn't counted for punches, only for some abilities.
The Thing|Embattled Leap jumps to an ally and reduces damage for himself and allies nearby; Battle Blitz jumps to an enemy and makes enemies in the landing area take more damage. Diving with no ally to leap back to is a mistake, and good players keep one leap for getting out rather than spending both at once.
The Thing|Yancy Street Charge launches enemies and stops their movement abilities near where it ends, so good players use it before or during an ally's ultimate so enemies can't escape. He can't be stunned while charging, but he's weak to stuns and burst damage once the charge ends.
The Thing|His ultimate, Clobberin' Time, launches and stuns everything in front of him; the best targets are crowds, especially ones with healers in them. He's weak against flying heroes, high ground and heroes who stay out of reach, such as Human Torch, Hela and Rocket.

The Hood|A brawl tank who can also dive at times. Hitting enemies with his pistols builds Demonic Energy; at full energy he enters Half-Demon State (fast rifles with lifesteal). His damage drops off sharply at range, so low damage often means he was poking from too far away.
The Hood|Mantle of Oblivion blocks attacks and turns the blocked damage into energy, which is where most of his damage blocked comes from. He can't use it during Half-Demon State, which is when he's easiest to kill.
The Hood|Abyssal Veil weakens damage and healing that pass through it, and enemies who walk through it take damage and are blinded, so it's good for cutting off healing to the enemy frontline. Void Walk makes him untargetable and is his main escape; using it with no plan to get out is a common reason he dies.
The Hood|He wins some 1v1s but it depends heavily on the opponent; he usually loses one to a Winter Soldier. His ultimate, Demon of the End, fires piercing shots and gives bonus health to him and nearby allies; missing the final shot, staying in it too long, or ulting into several heavy hitters wastes it. He's stationary while firing it, so he can be focus fired, and Magneto's ultimate counters it.

Venom|A dive tank who pressures the enemy backline and gives his teammates no protection; his damage blocked mostly comes from enemies hitting him while Symbiotic Resilience is active, so it can be high. He's at his best when allies follow up on his dives.
Venom|Symbiotic Resilience gives more bonus health the lower his health is. Besides his ultimate it's his only way to survive heavy burst, so good players always dive with it off cooldown. High deaths usually mean diving without it.
Venom|Frenzied Arrival dashes to a spot from a height and launches enemies near the landing point; Cellular Corrosion slows enemies in reach and damages those who don't break free. Good players perch on walls, let the enemy pass, then dive a Duelist or Strategist rather than the first enemy they see.
Venom|His ultimate, Feast of the Abyss, burrows him underground to move freely, then devours enemies above for damage based on their current health, giving him that much bonus health and reducing their healing. Like Hulk's ultimate it keeps him in a fight rather than getting him out. He's weak against crowd control.

Groot|A wall tank who controls space with two walls. Thornlash Wall strikes nearby enemies that Groot and his allies attack. Ironwood Wall gives Groot bonus health from damage dealt by him and nearby allies, and makes Groot (not his teammates) Unstoppable while he's near it. He also has a ranged Spore Bomb. With no dash or shield he's weak to crowd control, flyers and long-range heroes.
Groot|Good players put Thornlash Walls behind enemies to cut off their escape or between enemy healers and their team. Ironwood Wall is usually hidden behind cover to keep it alive, but its high health also lets it be placed forward to apply maximum pressure. Heavy ranged damage such as The Punisher's breaks his walls fast.
Groot|His ultimate, Strangling Prison, pulls enemies into one spot and holds them; it's best paired with an ally's area-damage ultimate.

Peni Parker|A defensive trap tank, much stronger defending a point than attacking. Standing on her webs heals her and her allies and speeds them up, so her value drops sharply off her web network.
Peni Parker|The Spider-Nest makes webs and drones; good players protect it with their body and move it when enemies start targeting it. Arachno-Mines are best hidden in webs or on walls and ceilings. Hitting an enemy who already has a Web-Tracer with her snare (right click) again immobilizes them briefly, and the snare can interrupt enemy ultimates such as Scarlet Witch's, Iron Man's and Doctor Strange's.
Peni Parker|Her ultimate isn't one of the strong ones; it's best for stalling a point and denying space. Used with the nest down, mines follow her trail; it's much weaker if the nest has been destroyed.

Emma Frost|A tank who switches between poke and brawl: she pokes with her beam and barrier, then goes into Diamond Form to fight up close. Her beam's damage builds up during a fight, so she's weakest at the start. She can't escape or reach high ground. Mind's Aegis (her barrier) can cut off enemy healing.
Emma Frost|Diamond Form makes her Unstoppable and immune to crowd control but turns off her beam and Psychic Spear. Good players save it for when a diver comes in, especially dive tanks and dive brawlers such as Iron Fist, Daredevil and Magik, and keep it as a looming threat; it's also used to cancel enemy ultimates or kill an out-of-position target. Using it while being kited wastes it.
Emma Frost|Carbon Crush grabs an enemy; good players grab where their team can see the target. Her ultimate, Psionic Seduction, damages and stuns enemies who stay in its cone and stops them from using ultimates, so it shuts down enemy defensive ultimates; it also works as a tempo ultimate when both teams' cooldowns are spent. Walls and shields block it.

Captain America|A mobile backline harasser, not a frontline tank, without much in the way of combos: his job is to be annoying in the enemy backline and pull out their cooldowns. Expect low damage blocked compared with shield tanks and lower damage than you'd think.
Captain America|His shield deflects projectiles. Liberty Rush is a charge for escaping or chasing, but he can be stunned from behind during it. Vibranium Saw also reveals invisible enemies.
Captain America|Common mistakes: trying to hold the frontline, getting isolated, staying in the enemy backline too long, and sprinting during a flank that should be quiet. His ultimate, Freedom Charge, gives allies along its path bonus health, speed and faster ultimate charge. He has no healing outside it, so steady damage forces him back.

Angela|A flying tank who pulls enemies away from their team and kills them one by one; strongest on maps with ledges for environmental kills. Expect lower damage: her kit is about mobility and crowd control. Enemies often save stuns for her, and Shielded Stance blocks damage while she flies.
Angela|Assassin's Charge grabs up to two enemies and carries them; it's best on out-of-position targets of any role. Common mistakes: diving into 1v6 fights, staying too long with her axes, and using movement abilities after Divine Judgement, which locks her out of everything except Wingblade Ascent.
Angela|Her ultimate throws her spear wrapped in ribbons that bind nearby enemies where it lands; she can leap to it, damaging enemies around it and creating a Divine Judgement zone. Phoenix counters it, Magneto soft-counters it, and Gambit hard-counters it with his cleanse.

Rogue|A mobile burst tank who can carry fights or dive. Her cooldowns are long and chain together, so a missed combo leaves her out of action for a while, which shows as deaths or low output. Poke teams that keep away from her are her biggest problem, and she needs another tank to hold the front.
Rogue|Ability Absorption knocks down a target and copies one of their abilities, stealing stats by role (health from Vanguards, damage from Duelists, healing from Strategists). It has many uses; many Rogues steal Duelist abilities for extra damage to burst through targets with her combos.
Rogue|Taking damage in Defensive Stance builds up her burst for Southern Brawl inside her combos, and Southern Brawl doubles as an escape; blocking when a stun is coming is a common mistake. Her ultimate, Heartbreaker, drains ultimate charge from enemies who are close to their ultimates, so using it when enemies have little charge wastes it.

Devil Dinosaur|A frontline tank with a huge body, which is his weakness. He can't be knocked back and heals over time from keeping Bleed on enemies, so his value grows in long fights and tank matchups.
Devil Dinosaur|Bleed sets up his kit: Savage Predation (a leap and grab) and Frenzied Feast (burst and bonus health) are weak without it, and using them on targets without Bleed is a common mistake. Impact Beam applies Bleed and slows at range but no longer brings down flyers.
Devil Dinosaur|Buddy Barrier is a shield that moves with him: it defends his team, blocks big hits and stops healing on an enemy he's grabbed. Good players check teammates are with them before using the ultimate, Devil-Beast Rampage; ulting in a chokepoint or bunched up wastes it. Once his barrier and leap are spent he's easy to kill.

# ---- Duelists ----
Black Panther|A dive Duelist: a good Black Panther has a lot of final hits compared with his damage, because he dives in to finish targets rather than dealing steady damage. Less total damage than a sustained-damage Duelist such as Winter Soldier (Bucky) is normal for him; judge him by final hits and KOs.

Daredevil|A melee dive and flank Duelist and one of the best 1v1 heroes: he picks off isolated targets and guards his backline against flankers. Judge him by KOs and final hits and by staying alive; lower total damage than a sustained-damage Duelist is normal. Accuracy doesn't matter for him: ignore it.
Daredevil|Radar Sense shows all invisible enemies (including Invisible Woman) and enemies through walls, but only at short range. Good players use it to find isolated targets and call out positions.
Daredevil|Sonic Pursuit is his engage, and its cooldown resets when he or an ally gets the kill, so good Daredevils chain picks. Devil's Latch pulls him to a target but doesn't stun, so good players save it as an escape. Objection! blocks frontal damage and reflects projectiles; using it to absorb a stun or burst is what keeps him alive.
Daredevil|He has almost no answer to flying or long-range heroes. Common mistakes: engaging with Latch and no escape left, getting caught alone, and playing too passively.
Daredevil|His ultimate, Let the Devil Out, damages enemies in his line of sight with a blind that gets stronger the longer it lasts, and gives him Fury the whole time.

Spider-Man|One of the hardest heroes to master: a burst melee flanker with no defensive tools and very low health, who wins quick 1v1s and leaves. Judge him by final hits and KOs on squishy targets, not total damage. Accuracy only counts some abilities, so don't read much into it.
Spider-Man|He has many combos (the basic one tags a target with Web-Cluster, pulls with Get Over Here! and finishes with Amazing Combo). A good Spider-Man knows which combo fits the moment in a fight, which stats can't show, so coach his choices about when to engage and when to leave, not combos.
Spider-Man|The biggest mistake is engaging with no Web-Swing charges left, which is usually where his deaths come from; staying grounded or in melee too long is the other. Stuns, Namor's turrets and teams with three Strategists counter him.

Scarlet Witch|Reworked recently: now a mid-range brawler and flanker. Chaos Marks are the core of her kit: her beam (Chaos Control) deals damage to marked enemies, and damaging marked enemies gives her bonus health, which is what lets her brawl. Scarlet Hex is a forward shockwave that damages and marks enemies.
Scarlet Witch|Dark Seal, landed on a target or the ground (or pressed again to create the field), stuns enemies in range on the first explosion, then slows them and keeps marking them; it's best in doorways, escape routes and fights where enemies are grouped.
Scarlet Witch|Mystic Projection's two invulnerable dashes are her escapes; using both and being left with none is a common reason she dies.
Scarlet Witch|Her ultimate is a flight that gives bonus health while pulling nearby enemies in and marking them with damage that grows over time, then ends in a massive area blast. It's best used to counter an enemy support ultimate or to keep enemies out of a space.

Psylocke|A stealth assassin who closes in, bursts a squishy target and leaves. She's decent at mid range but bad at long range. Judge her by final hits and KOs on Strategists and Duelists. Hitting crossbow shots shortens her cooldowns, so missing them slows her whole kit.
Psylocke|Wing Shurikens stick to enemies, deal damage and give her bonus health; recalling them is her burst damage. Good players keep one Psi-Blade Dash and Psychic Stealth available to engage or get out; deaths usually mean both were spent or a combo failed with no way out.
Psylocke|Her ultimate, Dance of the Butterfly, is best used to bait out a support ultimate or to put pressure on the enemy; using it at low health or in front of a stun wastes it.

Iron Fist|A brawler and diver who wins a lot of 1v1s thanks to his block. Accuracy doesn't matter for him: ignore it. Damage counts somewhat but isn't the main measure; judge him mostly by kills and staying alive.
Iron Fist|Dragon's Defense (his block) turns blocked damage into bonus health; he's vulnerable while it's down. K'un-Lun Kick both secures kills (it hits harder on low-health targets) and is his way out of the backline, so being without both kick and block can be fatal; good players decide whether a target is low enough to spend the kick on.
Iron Fist|After blocking, Yat Jee Chung Kuen shreds tanks and reduces the damage he takes, so fighting near several enemies isn't automatically a mistake, but doing it without block leaves him open to burst. Good Iron Fists dive in for a kill, and if it doesn't come, kick back to the team and pressure a tank with Yat Jee Chung Kuen. Harmony Recovery heals him but must be used out of sight.
Iron Fist|His ultimate, Living Chi, boosts his damage, speed and range and shortens his cooldowns; it's best used on tanks or Strategists.

Magik|A melee brawler and diver who bursts down targets and escapes through invincible Stepping Discs. Accuracy doesn't matter for her: ignore it. Part of her damage turns into bonus health, which is why she wins 1v1s; missing her first hits leaves her fragile.
Magik|Good players chain Stepping Discs into Demon's Rage, which summons a Limbo demon that keeps attacking its target, a big source of bonus health; Umbral Incursion with the demon can secure kills. Stepping Discs also dodge enemy ultimates.
Magik|Her ultimate, Darkchild, resets and powers up her abilities but gives no bonus health; using it in the open, at low health or alone gets her killed. A well-timed slash and dash during it can counter a support ultimate.
Magik|Winter Soldier, high-burst teams, Namor, Peni Parker and flying heroes counter her.

Wolverine|A tank-buster whose claw damage grows with the target's max health. Accuracy doesn't matter for him: ignore it. The most important part of playing him is grabbing an out-of-position target with Feral Leap and bringing them to his team; Rage matters less.
Wolverine|He can't grab The Thing or Devil Dinosaur, who can't be displaced. The biggest mistake is snatching an enemy into their own team or leaping from too far away. High-burst teams, flying teams and crowd control counter him; Invisible Woman's Force Physics can pull him out of a snatch.
Wolverine|His ultimate, Last Stand, launches enemies ahead, sweeps up others along his path and slams down at the landing point. It's best after grabbing a tank into their backline and ulting their supports and Duelists, or simply on the supports.

Hela|A long-range hitscan Duelist. Headshots are very important for her, though her value isn't only headshots; accuracy matters, so compare it with other hitscan Duelists such as The Punisher. She can also flank and take off-angles as long as Astral Flock (her invincible escape) is available.
Hela|Piercing Night fires thorns that detonate after a delay, good for breaking shields and bursting down targets. Soul Drainer's stun stops melee divers or sets up headshots. Shields and walls (Doctor Strange, Magneto, Groot) blunt her damage, and divers are her biggest threat; high deaths usually mean she was caught without Astral Flock.
Hela|Her ultimate, Goddess of Death, can save her after an aggressive flank and is good for pulling out enemy support ultimates. It has a noticeable startup, so it's best when enemy defensive ultimates are spent.

The Punisher|A sustained-damage Duelist who usually builds a wall of damage beside his team; compare his damage with other sustained-damage Duelists such as Winter Soldier. Low damage usually means he wasn't shooting or was shooting into shields.
The Punisher|Rifle for mid range and flying heroes; the shotgun is strong up close and against dive tanks, and suits a flanking playstyle: good players use ziplines to get into the backline and assassinate supports unseen. Scourge Grenade blocks vision and knocks him back, his escape from divers. His turret has a long startup, so placing it under fire is a mistake.
The Punisher|His ultimate, Final Judgment, frees him from crowd control when it starts. It's best used to pull out a support ultimate, to start a fight, or as a tempo ultimate; he uses it behind a shield tank because Magneto's ultimate hard-counters it. Divers and snipers that outrange him (Hela, Hawkeye) counter him.

Star-Lord|A mobile close-range flanker who uses rocket boots and short invincible dashes to reach the backline; strong in duels and against melee heroes, weak at long range. Aim matters a lot on him, so low accuracy can mean poor aim, though his damage also falls off at range.
Star-Lord|Stellar Shift has two charges and also reloads and gives bonus health; good players save one to dodge crowd control. Running out of both Stellar Shift and rocket fuel is how he dies. Good players flank from a different angle than their team.
Star-Lord|Blaster Barrage fires a frenzy of shots at enemies in range; combined with Rocket Propulsion it makes him hard to hit while dealing area damage to farm his ultimate, which can decide fights. His ultimate, Galactic Legend, locks on to targets and is hard-countered by Magneto's ultimate; using it into healing ultimates also wastes it.

Human Torch|A space controller and flanker. Flame Fields and Pyro-Prison walls push enemies off objectives and split them from their team. He can assassinate a target with two Blazing Blasts and a slam directly on them. He has no self-healing and is weak to hitscan heroes such as Hela, The Punisher and Adam Warlock.
Human Torch|Good players stack Blazing Blasts in threes for a bigger Pyro-Prison; prison plus slam, timed well, can kill through support ultimates. Flaming Meteor is also his escape, and slamming into a full-health team is a common way he dies.
Human Torch|His ultimate, Supernova, turns Flame Fields into fire tornadoes and reduces the damage he takes; it's best for funneling enemies into bad spots.

Moon Knight|Good on high ground and also when playing with his team. He farms ultimate charge by bouncing shots off tanks onto enemies behind them, so high damage partly from hitting tanks is expected. He's strongest against grouped teams.
Moon Knight|Ancient Ankh pulls enemies together and sets up his combos; good players place it out of the enemy's sight. Moonlight Hook has no invincibility, so it isn't a last-second dodge.
Moon Knight|Flying heroes, shield tanks (the Ankh breaks on barriers), fast divers, and teams that take off-angles on him with heroes like Hela or Phoenix punish him. His ultimate, Hand of Khonshu, is best paired with a stun or pull such as Groot's or Angela's, or on enemies whose escapes are spent.

Black Widow|Reworked in Season 9: now a flanker and close-range fighter rather than a full-time sniper. Her normal shots are fast, unscoped and can't one-shot; her scoped shooting comes only from her ultimate, Assassin's Focus, a few piercing shots. She sprints and super-jumps without a stamina bar.
Black Widow|Good Widows apply frontline pressure while taking off-angles and flanks at the right time. Widow's Bite Slam leaps to the ground for area damage in close-range combos; Edge Dancer zips in and kicks, but the kick no longer stuns, so she's still vulnerable to dive. Electro-Plasma Blast (her old ultimate) is now a regular exploding shot.

Black Cat|A mobile burst diver who finds isolated targets and escapes with her movement. She runs on Fortune, built by hitting enemies and spent on abilities and Gilded Deal items; she's strong against isolated enemies and weak in long, grouped fights.
Black Cat|Good players mainly buy Helm of Hades (a few seconds of invisibility, the most versatile engage and disengage) or Ring of Zona (a portal pair whose placement depends on where the enemy is); Chernobog's Crystal cleanses a stun but is less versatile. A common way she dies is running out of Fortune with no dashes left.
Black Cat|Her ultimate, Calling Card, marks every enemy and lets her dash between them; each KO refreshes it, so it's best when enemies are split up. Grouped teams and saved crowd control counter her.

Blade|A hybrid Duelist who can frontline, flank, poke or fight in melee. His core tool is healing reduction (Daywalker Dash with the sword, sword hits, his ultimate); good Blades put anti-heal on tanks, divers and any out-of-position target. His accuracy mixes sword and gun, so don't read much into it.
Blade|His gun fires a bullet that breaks apart midway and spreads like a shotgun, so it's most effective at mid range, not long or point-blank range. Scarlet Shroud is a short parry that blocks frontal damage and stuns and charges his dash.
Blade|Bloodline Awakening gives attack speed and lifesteal but reduces his own healing, so staying in it while taking extended burst can get him killed. He has low sustain in grouped fights. His ultimate, Thousand-Fold Slash, is best combined with an ally's crowd control.

Cyclops|A long-range hitscan Duelist with little damage falloff who wins at mid range against short-range heroes and can also take off-angles; accuracy matters, so compare it with other hitscan heroes.
Cyclops|Propulsion Burst knocks him and a target back and is his main escape; using it carelessly leaves him open to dives for a long cooldown. Good players use Optic Ascent for positioning and save Propulsion Burst for mid-fight escapes.
Cyclops|His ultimate, Ruby Rage, deals damage based on the target's max health; used alone, enemies walk out of it, so it's best after an ally's stun such as Groot's, The Thing's or Doctor Strange's.

Elsa Bloodstone|A close-range shotgun Duelist who snowballs: her Instinct ranks build from kills, damage and trap hits and reset when she dies, so dying early keeps her weak. The most important parts of playing her are hitting shots consistently and, above all, getting value from her ultimate.
Elsa Bloodstone|Smoky Snare is an invisible trap she can dash to from far away; with the Devil Dinosaur team-up its radius is large enough that placement matters much less. At high Instinct her dash has a very short cooldown and gives bonus health. Fighting at long range is a mistake, since her shotgun spreads.
Elsa Bloodstone|Her ultimate, Apex Predator, summons a monster that grabs enemies and drags them back toward her; summoning it into heavy enemy ultimates gets it destroyed.

Gorr/Gorr the God Butcher|A melee dive Duelist who darts in and out. Accuracy doesn't matter for him: ignore it. His Black Berserkers (up to three) give him bonus health, damage and teleport points, so his survival depends on keeping them alive and keeping one safe as an escape.
Gorr/Gorr the God Butcher|Living Abyss is an invulnerable dash and Shadow Harvest teleports him back to a Berserker; good players place Berserkers before committing. Failing the first engage drains his resources and leaves him weak. He has a large hitbox and is weak to burst damage, poke, knockbacks and slows, and to enemies who destroy his Berserkers.
Gorr/Gorr the God Butcher|His ultimate, Twilight of the Gods, powers up all his Berserkers; he needs his team around him during it to keep him alive.

Hawkeye|A long-range sniper: headshots matter enormously, so accuracy is meaningful. He's very strong or very weak depending on whether he lands his shots, and plays from off-angles and high ground, moving between spots rather than staying in one.
Hawkeye|Hypersonic Arrow ignores shields and knocks down flying heroes; Crescent Slash knocks back melee divers. Fast flankers and constant pressure are his biggest threats. Holding a charged arrow for a teammate's stun is how he gets reliable kills.
Hawkeye|His ultimate, Hunter's Sight, creates afterimages that pass damage to the real enemies. Magneto's ultimate hard-counters it, and enemies can beat it by getting out of his line of sight.

Winter Soldier|Usually deals more total damage than dive Duelists such as Black Panther, so compare his damage with other sustained-damage Duelists rather than with divers.

# ---- Strategists ----
Rocket Raccoon|His main value is healing and staying alive: he can run up walls and has dashes to escape. His healing orbs bounce and pass through allies, so good players aim at teammates' feet and heal around corners.
Rocket Raccoon|His gun is much more effective at short range than at medium or long range, so shooting from a distance lowers both his accuracy and his damage.
Rocket Raccoon|Many Rocket players stand farther from their team than other Strategists because his healing orb reaches a long way. Low accuracy and low damage on Rocket usually reflect that distance, not poor aim, so coach it as a positioning trade-off (when it's safe to move closer and add damage) rather than as an aim problem. Damage matters most when the team is healthy and for breaking deployables.
Rocket Raccoon|His Battle Rebirth Beacon (revives the next nearby teammate who dies) and his ultimate (an amplifier that boosts damage and heals) don't show on the scoreboard. Common mistake: using both dashes early and getting caught with no escape.

Luna Snow|Her value is both healing and damage. High damage with low healing can mean a flanking Luna, a high-risk, high-reward playstyle; judge it by whether her team still had enough healing and by her deaths.
Luna Snow|She has little mobility and usually plays behind cover; deaths often mean she was too exposed. Absolute Zero and Ice Arts heal her, and Number One Idol puts Share the Stage's snowflake on herself so she heals while dealing damage.
Luna Snow|Good players save Absolute Zero's freeze to cancel an enemy ultimate or stop a diver.
Luna Snow|Her ultimate switches between a healing aura and a damage boost. The worst mistake is letting an ally die while in damage mode. Share the Stage is best on Duelists and divers.

Invisible Woman|Her healing and damage come from the same piercing orbs that fly back to her. Lining up allies and enemies gives both, so she can post good healing and damage at once; if both are low, she probably wasn't getting good lines.
Invisible Woman|Her healing range is shorter than most Strategists', so she plays close behind the frontline. She's decent against dive but bad against Daredevil, who can see her through invisibility. Check the enemy team for him.
Invisible Woman|Invisibility heals her but stops her attacking, so time spent invisible lowers her output: a survival-versus-output trade-off.
Invisible Woman|She can also put Guardian Shield on herself, which helps her heal herself and survive big area ultimates such as Magneto's or Iron Man's.
Invisible Woman|Good players put Guardian Shield on the most threatened ally and recall it before it breaks, use Force Physics to peel divers or pull in enemies who push too far, and time Psionic Vortex with a teammate's area-damage ultimate.
Invisible Woman|Her ultimate makes allies invisible and heals them, but she is vulnerable to focus fire in general: any team that bursts her down at once can beat it.

Jubilee|A Strategist who mixes healing and damage. Her output depends on keeping her kit cycling (Blooming Ball, detonating Sparkle Marks on enemies to start rapid fire), so healing and damage should both be solid; if both are low she was probably idle or out of range.
Jubilee|Good players recall and reuse Blooming Ball instead of letting it expire, save Sparking Sprint to quickly charge up Blooming Ball rather than as an escape, and put Blooming Ball on themselves when they're being dived.
Jubilee|She has poor mobility, so dive teams and cleansers such as White Fox and Black Cat counter her; deaths against dive are often about positioning. Her ultimate, Firework Finale, heals allies and damages enemies in an orbit around her: a tighter orbit spins faster, a wider one covers more ground.
